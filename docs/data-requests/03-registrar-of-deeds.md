## Request to discuss what title information the Registry can supply or confirm electronically, and how rates clearances and sale notifications should pass between the Registry and the council

**1. Why we are writing.** Vungu Rural District Council is introducing a digital land register. The Registry holds title; the council holds its own records (the stand, its land category, permits, notices and what is owed on it). The register will not record who owns a parcel unless the Registry or the owner's title document says so. We are writing to learn what the Registry can do, not to ask it to change how it works.

**2. What we ask.**

1. Whether the Registry can confirm, on request and electronically, for a named stand or farm: the registered owner, the title deed number and date, and any registered conditions or caveats. If so, through what channel, to whom, and at what fee.
2. If the Registry cannot give this electronically, what a council officer should lodge or present to obtain it, so that the council can build its procedure around the real one.
3. How the Registry would like to receive the council's clearance certificate required before transfer of land in the council's area (Rural District Councils Act, section 109), so that the council's certificate can be checked for authenticity by the Registry.
4. How the Registry would like to receive, or whether it needs to receive, the council's notification of a sale of land in the council's area (section 116), and what form it should take.
5. Whether the Registry would be willing to name one officer with whom the council's land officer can resolve differences between the two sets of records.

**3. Personal data.** An owner's name and identity details are personal data. We ask only for what the purpose needs: the owner's name and the deed reference, not identity-document numbers or addresses unless the Registry considers them necessary to identify the owner. The council will restrict who sees them to its land and rates officers, and will show the owner's name to the public only where the law makes the register public. We will follow any conditions the Registry sets.

**4. How we will use it.** Whatever we receive will be held as "as recorded by the Registry on [date]", with the deed reference. The council will not treat its own copy as title, and will not alter it. Where the council's records disagree with the Registry's, the council will say so and send the matter to the Registry.

**5. Timing.** We would be grateful for a meeting or a written reply by [date, suggest 30 days].

**6. Contact.** [Name, post, telephone, email].
