## Request for the instruments, directions and requirements that govern the council's planning functions

**1. Why we are writing.** Vungu Rural District Council is the local planning authority for its area under the Regional, Town and Country Planning Act [Chapter 29:12]. We are setting out, in writing, how the council receives, assesses and decides applications for development permits, who may decide what, and what the council must send to or consult with the Ministry and the Department. We want each step to rest on the Act, on an instrument made under it, or on the Department's own requirements, and not on practice or on secondary sources. On several points we cannot find the instrument and would rather ask than assume.

**2. What we ask for.**

1. **The authority under which the council acts as a local planning authority.** The statutory instrument or instruments under section 10(1)(b) of the Act by which the Minister has authorised the council to be a local planning authority for the purposes of Part IV (master and local plans) and Part VI (subdivisions and consolidations), with the extent and conditions the notice specifies. If there is none, please tell us which of those functions the council may exercise and who exercises the others for the council's area.
2. **Directions and guides in force.** Any directions given to the council under sections 29, 69 or 70 of the Act, including any direction referring applications or classes of applications to the Minister or restricting permits for a type of development, and any advisory notes or guides issued under section 69(2) that the council must take into account. We would include the Department's guidelines on development management and the standards on which the Ministry's *Development Management (Control) Handbook* (2021) relies, with a statement of which apply to a rural council, for example to business centres, growth points, homesteads and farms.
3. **Returns and information.** Whether the Ministry or the Department requires the council to send periodic returns, copies of permits or refusals, extracts of its development register, or notice of plans and layouts, and if so in what form and by what dates.
4. **Layouts on Communal Land and State land.** The procedure, documents, consultations and time the Department requires for the approval of a proposed layout for the subdivision of State land or Communal Land under section 43, and the status of any layout submitted by or for the council.
5. **Plans.** The Department's requirements for consulting it before the council prepares a local plan where there is no operative master plan (section 17), and the terms of reference, standards and approval steps for master and local plans in the council's area, including the Department's part and the Minister's.
6. **The council's planning department.** Any instruction or guidance from the Ministry on establishing a department of spatial planning and land management within a rural district council, and how it bears on the post of Executive Officer (Planning), its title, its powers and its reporting line. We have seen a reference to such a call and ask whether it applies to the council.
7. **The current law.** The current consolidated text of the Act with every amendment since 2016, and the Regional, Town and Country Planning Regulations in force (the contents of an application, fees, procedures and plans), if the Ministry holds them in a form it can supply.
8. **A contact.** The name, post and contact details of the Department's officer who deals with the council (we understand a Provincial Planning Officer for the Midlands), and confirmation of the correct name of the Department and of the officer to whom we should address planning matters.

**3. Form.** Scanned copies are sufficient. For an instrument, please include its Gazette reference and date. Where a document exists in digital form we would be grateful for that.

**4. How we will use it.** We will record the instrument or requirement against each function, decision and referral in the council's planning workflow, so that every decision can be traced to the authority for it. Where we cannot find an instrument for something the council has been doing, we will say "the council's own practice" and not present it as law. We will not treat guidance, including the Handbook, as binding where the Act does not make it so.

**5. Timing.** We would be grateful for a reply by [date, suggest 30 days]. If any item is better directed elsewhere, we would be grateful to be told where.

**6. Contact.** [Name, post, telephone, email].
