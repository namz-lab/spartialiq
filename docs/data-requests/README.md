## Four rules the letters follow

1. **Ask for exactly what we can use, in a form we can name.** A request for "your data" is not answerable. Each letter lists the fields, the coordinate system and the file formats.
2. **The custodian keeps its truth.** We ask to hold a dated, referenced, read-only copy of what the custodian holds, and to say where it came from. We do not ask to edit it.
3. **Say how personal data is handled, and ask only for what the purpose needs.** The Registry and finance letters involve personal data; they ask for the minimum and say who sees it.
4. **Offer something back.** Where the council finds an error while reconciling (a parcel that disagrees with its plan, a ward that does not match), it will report it to the custodian.

## What the letters do not say
They do not cite provisions that have not been checked against the Gazetted text. The Rural District Councils Act sections they rely on (3, 8, 109, 116) were read from an amended consolidation; the legal officer should confirm the wording and numbering before signature. The Land Survey Act and the Land Survey (General) Regulations are named but not quoted.
